FAMELO Teaching Dataset Codebook

Background and codebook for the SOCI 210 FAMELO teaching dataset.

About the FAMELO data

The Family Migration and Early Life Outcomes (FAMELO) project is a household-based study of children, young people, and their families in three settings: Jalisco, Mexico; Gaza Province, Mozambique; and the Chitwan Valley, Nepal. The study was designed to examine young people’s lives in places experiencing substantial social and economic change and high levels of migration.

FAMELO collected information from focal children and their primary caregivers, as well as information about their households. The SOCI 210 teaching dataset uses Wave 2 data only and contains a selected set of variables focused on the transition to adulthood, including education, work, health, family circumstances, migration, relationships, marriage, and young people’s orientations toward adulthood. The study sites are not intended to be nationally representative, so findings should be interpreted as describing these study settings rather than Mexico, Mozambique, or Nepal as a whole.

The SOCI 210 data file

The SOCI 210 data file is a reduced teaching dataset rather than the complete FAMELO dataset. Each row represents one focal child. The dataframe contains 7,876 observations and 42 variables selected for the analyses used in the course. Variables may come from the focal child’s interview, the caregiver interview, household information, or constructed measures.

Reading coded categories

Some variables use numbers to represent categories. These numbers are category codes rather than measured quantities. You do not need to memorize the codes; use this codebook to check what they represent.

Education variables ending in _isced use ordered categories based on the International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED). These numbers are not years of schooling and should not be treated as an equal-interval quantitative measure. The corresponding labeled education variables are usually easier to use in tables and graphs.

Missing values

Missing values appear as NA in R. NA means that information is not available for that observation. For some variables, especially questions about romantic relationships and later adolescent experiences, a value may be missing because the question did not apply to that respondent.
